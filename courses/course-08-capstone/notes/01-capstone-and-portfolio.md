# Capstone, Case Study Tracks & Portfolio

## What the capstone practices
Taking a real dataset through Ask, Prepare, Process, Analyze, Share and Act: stating a business task, importing data, documenting cleaning, analyzing, visualizing, summarizing findings, recommending actions, and publishing a case study.

## Two tracks
| Track | What it is |
|---|---|
| **A** | Given questions and datasets (Cyclistic bike-share or Bellabeat fitness tracker) |
| **B** | Choose your own question and public dataset; define all requirements yourself |

**My choice: Track B**, healthcare cost analysis aligned with health plan data analyst roles.

## Guiding questions by phase
| Phase | Key tasks |
|---|---|
| Ask | Identify the problem and stakeholders; establish metrics |
| Prepare | Collect and store data; check organization and credibility (ROCCC); licensing and privacy |
| Process | Choose tools; check errors; transform types; document cleaning |
| Analyze | Aggregate, format, calculate; find trends and relationships |
| Share | Tell the story with effective, accessible visualizations |
| Act | Final conclusion, next steps, additional data, portfolio |

## ROCCC (data credibility)
**R**eliable · **O**riginal · **C**omprehensive · **C**urrent · **C**ited

## Portfolio
- Platforms: GitHub, Kaggle, Tableau Public, personal website
- A case study should show the question, process, code, visuals and conclusions
- Write for a hiring manager: lead with the business problem and findings
- Keep it reproducible: data source links, queries, cleaning log

## Lesson from my capstone
Profiling the first dataset showed it was randomly generated (every segment cost the same), so I replaced it. Checking data credibility *before* analysis saved the project.
