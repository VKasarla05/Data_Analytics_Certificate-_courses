# Data-Driven vs. Data-Inspired Decisions

## The core distinction
- **Data-driven decisions** — using facts alone to arrive at a decision. Powerful when data quality/quantity is sufficient, but risky if the data is incomplete or biased.
- **Data-inspired decisions** — layer in qualitative context (feelings, experience, related concepts) on top of the data. Helps avoid the blind spots of a purely data-driven approach.

**Key reminder:** data doesn't make decisions — it improves them.

## Example: A/B testing (data-driven)
Splitting website visitors between an old and new layout for two weeks, then comparing
widget sales, is a clean example of letting data alone decide.

## Example: CSAT improvement (data-inspired)
A support center uses a 1–10 satisfaction score *plus* qualitative descriptions and staff
interviews to decide what to improve — the score alone wouldn't tell the full story.

## When data-driven decisions go wrong

**New Coke (1985)** — Taste tests with 200,000 people showed people preferred the new
formula over Pepsi. Classic Coke was pulled from shelves based on that data alone. The
company lost tens of millions because the data never accounted for how attached customers
were to the *original* product — the data was accurate but incomplete.

**Mars Climate Orbiter (1999)** — NASA lost a $125M spacecraft not because the data was
wrong, but because of a units mismatch: one team used metric (newtons), another used
imperial (pounds), and nobody caught the inconsistency. A reminder that accurate data
processed with a wrong assumption still produces a wrong decision — collaboration and
communication matter as much as the data itself.

## My takeaway
Both failures reinforce something from Course 1: the Ask phase and stakeholder alignment
matter *before* the data ever gets analyzed. A "correct" dataset can still lead to a bad
decision if the interpretation or communication around it breaks down.
