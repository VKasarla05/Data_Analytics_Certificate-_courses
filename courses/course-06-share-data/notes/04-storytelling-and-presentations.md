# Data Storytelling & Presentations

## Three elements of data storytelling
1. **Characters:** the people affected by the story (stakeholders, members)
2. **Setting:** the context: what's going on, why now
3. **Plot / conflict:** the problem the data reveals
4. **Big reveal:** the insight that resolves it
5. **Aha moment:** the recommendation or action

## Engaging the audience
- **Know your audience:** what do they know, what do they need to know?
- **Spotlighting:** scan your findings for the ideas that matter most, then build the story around them
- **Lead with the answer**, then support it with evidence

## Presentation structure
1. Title and purpose
2. The business task / question
3. Key findings (one chart per slide, title = finding)
4. Recommendation
5. Next steps and limitations
6. Appendix (methods, data sources)

## Delivering the presentation
- Use the **McCandless method**: introduce the graphic by name, answer obvious questions before they're asked, state the insight, call out data that supports it, tell the audience why it matters
- Speak to the audience, not the slides
- Pause after key points

## Handling Q&A
- Listen to the whole question
- Repeat or clarify it
- Answer briefly; if unsure, say so and follow up
- Keep answers tied to the data

## Caveats and limitations
Always state them clearly: data source, time period, assumptions, what the data can't show.
