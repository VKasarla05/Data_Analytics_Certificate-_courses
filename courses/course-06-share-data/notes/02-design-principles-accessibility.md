# Design Principles & Accessibility

## Design thinking for visualizations
1. **Empathize:** who is the audience and what do they need?
2. **Define:** what exactly should the visual show?
3. **Ideate:** sketch several options
4. **Prototype:** build a draft
5. **Test:** get feedback and revise

## Elements of effective design
- **Clear meaning:** the insight is obvious
- **Sophisticated use of contrast:** highlight what matters, mute the rest
- **Refined execution:** clean layout, aligned elements, no clutter

## Design principles
- **Choose the right visual** for the question
- **Optimize the data-ink ratio:** remove gridlines, borders and effects that don't add meaning
- **Use orientation effectively:** horizontal bars for long category names
- **Color:** consistent, purposeful, limited palette
- **Numbers of things:** don't overload one chart

## Headlines and labels
- **Title = the finding** ("Hospital stays: 7% of claims, 73% of costs"), not the chart type ("Cost by care type")
- **Subtitle:** context (time period, source)
- **Label data directly** instead of relying on a legend
- **Axis titles and units** always included

## Accessibility
- **Colorblind-safe palettes** (e.g., blue/orange; avoid red vs. green)
- **Never rely on color alone:** add labels, patterns or text
- **Alt text** describing the finding for screen readers
- **Readable fonts and sizes**, high contrast
- **Simplify:** one message per chart
- **Text alternatives:** a written summary alongside visuals
