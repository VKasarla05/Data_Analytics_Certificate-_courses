# The Six Phases of the Data Analysis Life Cycle

Expanded notes on each phase, with the specific actions that define it.

## 1. Ask
- Clarify stakeholder expectations: who they are, what they want, why, and when.
- Define the problem by comparing the current state to the ideal state.
- Turn that gap into specific, answerable questions.

## 2. Prepare
- Identify and locate the data needed to answer the questions.
- Check the data for bias — results should be objective, fair, and impartial.

## 3. Process
- Clean the data: remove errors, duplicates, and inconsistencies.
- Transform data into a more useful format.
- Combine datasets where needed for completeness.
- Remove outliers that would skew results.
- Verify the cleaned data is complete and correct before moving on.

## 4. Analyze
- Turn prepared data into actionable information.
- Common tools: spreadsheets, SQL, Python.

## 5. Share
- Communicate findings so stakeholders can make data-driven decisions.
- Visualization is the key tool here — it's how people actually understand the data.

## 6. Act
- Put insights to work: recommendations, decisions, next steps.
- In the certificate program, this phase culminates in a capstone case study.

## My takeaway
Process (cleaning/verifying) and Share (visualization) are the two phases most likely to
make or break trust in an analysis — bad data or a confusing chart can undo good analysis
work in the Analyze phase.
