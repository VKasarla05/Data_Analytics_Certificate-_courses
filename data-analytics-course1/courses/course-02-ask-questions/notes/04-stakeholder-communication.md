# Stakeholder Communication & Project Management

## Three common stakeholder groups
- **Executive team** — wants the headline first; limited time, high-level decisions. Lead
  with answers, keep details in an appendix.
- **Customer-facing team** — brings their own asks/hypotheses; important to let the data
  tell the story rather than confirming what they hope to find.
- **Data science team** — analysts, scientists, engineers working together; different
  people often analyze different angles of the same problem before combining findings.

## Working effectively with stakeholders
- **Discuss goals** — a request is usually tied to a bigger goal; ask about it.
- **Feel empowered to say no** — if a timeline or ask isn't realistic, push back
  respectfully and explain why.
- **Plan for the unexpected** — build buffer time into timelines for roadblocks.
- **Know your project** — track discussions and be ready to explain how the work connects
  to broader goals.
- **Start with words and visuals** — avoid the "illusion of agreement" by pairing a
  description with a quick visual early, before too much work is invested in one direction.
- **Communicate often** — regular updates, milestones, and a change-log (a chronological
  list of project modifications).

## The four audience questions
Before communicating any finding, answer:
1. Who is your audience?
2. What do they already know?
3. What do they need to know?
4. How can you best communicate what they need to know?

## Leading meetings
**Before:** identify the objective, involve the right participants, prepare data/visuals,
distribute an agenda (start/end time, location, objectives, background material).

**During:** make introductions, present the data, discuss implications, take notes,
summarize next steps.

**After:** distribute a recap, confirm next steps/timeline, ask for feedback.

## My takeaway
For my healthcare project, I'm treating my case study README + weekly logs as a
lightweight "change-log" — documenting decisions and progress the way I would for a real
stakeholder, even though the audience right now is really just me (and future recruiters).
